A bead chain pouring from a beaker rises above the rim in an arch.
This chain fountain can't be explained by chain tension alone, and the pile in the beaker has to push up.

The weight of the chain hanging outside the beaker pulls the rest out. If tension alone started the resting chain moving, the calculated arch height would be zero. Treat a short piece of chain as a rod, and lifting one end drives the other end into the pile, which pushes it back up. In the experiment, the arch stood about 14% as high as the drop and grew in proportion to it.

Visuals are explanatory models and may not be to scale. Narration is synthetic.
