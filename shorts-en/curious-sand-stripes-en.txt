Pour mixed grains into a narrow gap and granular stratification builds stripes on its own.
Grain size segregation and a difference in angle of repose decide whether the layers appear.

Pour equal volumes of small glass beads and larger sugar crystals, and the slope keeps collapsing in sudden slides. Big grains roll more easily over small ones, so the small grains stop first at the bottom and the big ones stop on top. The stopping line climbs back up the slope and finishes one pair of layers. If the bigger grains have the smaller angle of repose, you get two zones and no stripes.
