In a steel ball impact on fine, fluffy sand, a granular jet shoots upward.
A void collapse starts the thin jet, and squeezed air drives the thick part behind it.

The ball leaves a hole, and the weight of the surrounding sand squeezes it shut. Sand meeting at the center splits up and down, sending a thin, fast jet upward, and a little sand carrying a share of the energy can rise above the drop height. X-ray imaging showed a thin front and a thick back. Lowering the air pressure weakened the thick part and left the thin jet nearly unchanged.

Visuals are explanatory models and may not be to scale. Narration is synthetic.
