Why do ferrofluid spikes rise when a magnet comes close?
A magnetic field grows surface bumps while surface tension and gravity push them flat.

The field crowds into each bump, and the liquid gets pulled in after it, so the bump rises. Gravity and surface tension push back, but past a threshold field the bumps keep growing into spikes. Under a wide, even field the spikes form a hexagon pattern, with a calculated spacing of about 1 cm for one fluid. When the field weakens, the spikes collapse and the surface goes flat.

Visuals are explanatory models and may not be to scale. Narration is synthetic.

Sources: Cowley, Rosensweig (1967), Journal of Fluid Mechanics / Gollwitzer et al. (2009), New Journal of Physics
