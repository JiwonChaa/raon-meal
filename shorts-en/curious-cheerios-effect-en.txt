Why does floating cereal clump together in a bowl of milk?
The meniscus effect, shaped by surface tension, sends each piece up its neighbor's slope.

Milk climbs a little up each piece of cereal, so the surface around it slopes upward. Cereal's buoyancy beats its weight, so each piece climbs the slope its neighbor made until they touch. A heavy metal tack presses the surface down and slides downhill, and next to a light plastic head, the two drift apart. Objects that bend the surface the same way gather, and opposite bends separate.
