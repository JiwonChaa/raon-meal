A reverse sprinkler, the famous Feynman sprinkler, slowly turns the opposite way when it sucks water in.
The centrifugal effect in its bent tubes is what flips the direction.

When it sucks, the clear jets form inside the round hub at the center. Water leaving a bent tube has its fastest part shifted to the outside of the curve, so the two jets in the hub hit slightly off-center. As the water picks up a swirl, the sprinkler gets pushed the other way. Turn the suction down and the flow gets more symmetric, leaving only a weak jitter with no clear direction.

Sources: Wang et al. (2024), Physical Review Letters
