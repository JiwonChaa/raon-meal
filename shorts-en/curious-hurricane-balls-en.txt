With hurricane balls, two joined steel balls spin into a blur under air from a straw.
These spinning steel balls get so fast because rolling without slipping loses little energy to friction.

The air pushes the balls along their direction of spin and keeps adding energy. At high speed one ball lifts, and the one on the table rolls without sliding. The contact point has zero speed at each instant, so sliding friction takes little energy, and the balls keep turning for minutes after the air stops. In one air-jet test, a camera caught them turning 2,449 times a minute.

Visuals are explanatory models and may not be to scale. Narration is synthetic.
