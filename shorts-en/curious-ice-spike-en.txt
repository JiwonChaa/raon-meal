Why do ice spikes grow out of an ice cube tray?
The push comes from water expansion, which forces liquid through a tiny hole and builds a hollow ice tube.

The surface freezes from the edges until one small hole is left. As the water below freezes and expands, it's forced up through that hole, freezes at the rim, and extends a tube until the tube seals. In one experiment, about 0.6 mg of salt per liter of water cut the odds of a spike in half. How impurities stop the growth is still a hypothesis.

Visuals are explanatory models and may not be to scale. Narration is synthetic.

Sources: Libbrecht, Lui (2004), Journal of Glaciology
