A falling chimney can snap in midair before it reaches the ground.
The cause is a bending moment from the internal forces that hold its rotational motion together.

To turn as one piece, the higher parts have to speed up more, and above two-thirds of the height gravity alone can't keep up. The lower section drags the upper section forward, and that drag bends the chimney. The ground-facing side stretches until it can't take the tension, and a crack opens there first. The exact spot depends on material and structure, but it's usually between a third and half of the height.
