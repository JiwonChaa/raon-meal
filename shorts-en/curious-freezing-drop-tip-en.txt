Why does a freezing water drop grow a pointed tip?
As it freezes from the bottom, water expansion pushes the remaining liquid upward.

Almost no heat leaves through the sides of the ice, so the freezing front meets the ice surface at a right angle. As that front narrows, the added volume gets pushed up and the top closes into a cone. The measured tip angle is about 139°, and it stayed the same when the plate temperature or the drop's flatness changed. A model with no fitted numbers predicts 131°, at the low edge of the error range.

Sources: Marín et al. (2014), Physical Review Letters
