The coin wobble gets faster as a spinning coin settles toward the table.
For a rolling disk, a smaller tilt makes the contact point circle the table faster.

As the coin loses energy, its center of mass drops and its tilt shrinks. Holding a smaller tilt takes a faster-circling contact point, so the rattle speeds up. A mark on the coin's face does the opposite, slowing until it looks almost still. Papers still disagree on whether rolling friction, slipping, or air drag takes most of the energy.

Visuals are explanatory models and may not be to scale. Narration is synthetic.
